What can and can't go in a skip

Waste type matters as much as size. Operators tip loads at licensed facilities such as the Albury Waste Management Centre in Hamilton Valley, and each type of waste costs a different amount to dispose of.

Usually accepted

  • General household waste: furniture, toys, clothes, boxes, cupboard contents and general junk.
  • Green waste: gum branches, prunings, autumn leaves, lawn clippings and hedge trimmings (often cheaper on its own, with no dirt or rubbish mixed in).
  • Renovation waste: timber, plasterboard, old cabinets, carpet, tiles and fittings.
  • Heavy or clean fill: soil, sand, concrete, bricks, rock and pavers (often needs its own bin and has a lower weight limit).
  • Scrap metal: old roofing iron, fencing, heaters and outdoor furniture.

Not accepted, or extra charge

  • Asbestos and fibro: never in a normal skip (see below).
  • Tyres: usually banned or charged per tyre.
  • Car and household batteries: a fire risk in a skip.
  • Gas bottles and aerosols: can explode when compacted.
  • Paint, oils, chemicals and liquids: including pool chemicals, sump oil, paint and garden sprays.
  • Mattresses: often accepted for an extra fee.
  • Food waste, medical waste and e-waste: check first. Many operators won't take TVs, computers or fluorescent tubes.

A note on asbestos

Many older Albury homes and sheds have fibro linings, eaves or fences that may contain asbestos. In NSW, removing more than 10 square metres of bonded (non-friable) asbestos needs a licensed removalist, and any friable asbestos needs a Class A licence. See SafeWork NSW's asbestos guidance. If you suspect asbestos, say so on the quote form. Never hide it in a general skip.

Dropping things off yourself

AlburyCity's Albury Waste Management Centre is at 565 Mudge Street, Hamilton Valley. Council lists it as open Monday to Friday 7am to 5pm and weekends 9am to 4pm, closed Good Friday and Christmas Day. It accepts small quantities of asbestos from residents only, triple-wrapped and sealed; bigger loads must be handled by a licensed removalist. Call the centre on (02) 6043 5860 to confirm requirements, and check AlburyCity's website for fees and accepted items.

Putting a skip on the street: AlburyCity rules

Last updated: 29 October 2026

On your own property

Your own driveway, yard or a firm patch of ground is the easiest place for a skip. Council's approvals cover skips on a footpath, road or other public place, so a bin wholly on your property generally doesn't need them. Put planks under the bin if you're worried about your driveway.

On the road, footpath or nature strip

AlburyCity's temporary road and footpath use application covers skip bins placed on a footpath or road. Placing a waste container in a public place is also an activity that needs approval under section 68 of the Local Government Act 1993. Council doesn't publish one fixed skip fee, so ask when you apply.

Before you book a skip on the street, check with AlburyCity on (02) 6023 8111 or info@alburycity.nsw.gov.au, or visit the AlburyCity website. Mention it on the quote form too.

Wherever the skip goes, keep it clear of driveways, hydrants, bus stops and footpaths, and don't fill it above the rim.

Skip bin vs trailer hire vs DIY tip runs

Three common ways to get rid of a big load of rubbish in Albury, compared honestly. The right one depends on how much you have and how much time you want to spend on it.

Skip bin

Effort
Delivered to your driveway or site. You load it at your own pace and the operator collects it.
Trips to the tip
None for you.
What you can load
Most household, garden and building waste, depending on the skip type you book. Some items are banned (see what can go in a skip).
Who handles disposal
The skip operator.
Best for
Clean-outs, renovations and jobs that take a few days.

Trailer hire

Effort
You need a vehicle with a tow bar. You load, drive, then unload at the tip yourself.
Trips to the tip
One per trailer load, so bigger jobs can mean several trips.
What you can load
Limited by the trailer size and what your vehicle can safely and legally tow.
Who handles disposal
You do, at the tip, following its fees and sorting rules each trip.
Best for
A medium one-off load when you already have a tow bar and spare time.

DIY tip runs

Effort
Loading and unloading your own car or ute every run.
Trips to the tip
Usually the most, because a car or ute holds the least.
What you can load
Whatever fits safely in your vehicle. Bulky items often won't.
Who handles disposal
You do, on every run.
Best for
Small amounts, or items your local tip or recycling centre takes separately.
