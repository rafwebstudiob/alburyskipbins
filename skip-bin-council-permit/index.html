Skip bin permits in Albury: AlburyCity rules

A skip on your own driveway usually doesn't need council approval. A skip on the road, footpath or nature strip is different. Here's what AlburyCity says and what to check before you book.

Last checked 24 October 2026

The short answer

On your own land, generally no. On a footpath or road, yes: AlburyCity's temporary road and footpath use application covers skip bins, and placing waste in a public place needs section 68 approval.

On your own property

Your own driveway, yard or a firm patch of ground is the easiest place for a skip. Council's approvals cover skips on a footpath, road or other public place, so a bin wholly on your property generally doesn't need them. Put planks under the bin if you're worried about your driveway.

On the road, footpath or nature strip

AlburyCity's temporary road and footpath use application covers skip bins placed on a footpath or road. Placing a waste container in a public place is also an activity that needs approval under section 68 of the Local Government Act 1993. Council doesn't publish one fixed skip fee, so ask when you apply.

Before you book a skip on the street, check with AlburyCity on (02) 6023 8111 or info@alburycity.nsw.gov.au, or visit the AlburyCity website. Mention it on the quote form too.

What to ask council before you book

  1. Does my spot need a temporary road and footpath use approval, a section 68 approval, or both?
  2. What does it cost this financial year, and how long does assessment take?
  3. How long can the skip stay, and are there conditions such as lights, reflectors or a set distance from the kerb?
  4. Are there extra rules for a busy road, a bus stop or a heritage area?

Ask for the answers in writing or note the date and who you spoke to.

Placing the skip safely

Keep the skip clear of driveways, hydrants, bus stops and drains, and don't fill above the rim. If it's on the footpath, leave room for people to walk past.

Where this information comes from

  • AlburyCity: temporary road and footpath use
  • ABLIS: Approval to place waste in a public place (Albury City Council)
  • NSW Planning Portal: section 68 approvals

Council rules, hours and fees change. Check with the council or facility before you rely on them.
