Rubbish Removal Albury: Skip Bins vs Pick-up

In short: for a big clean-up, a skip bin is often the easier way to remove household or green waste in Albury. For small amounts, the Albury Waste Management Centre and AlburyCity's four free waste vouchers a year are worth knowing about.

The main ways to get rid of household and garden rubbish in Albury, what each one takes, and when a skip bin makes more sense.

Last updated: 27 October 2026

Your options for rubbish removal in Albury

In short: For a big clean-up, a skip bin is usually the simplest way to remove household or garden waste. For a load or two, AlburyCity's free waste vouchers at the Albury Waste Management Centre may be enough.

Most Albury households have three ways to get rid of more rubbish than fits in their bins:

  1. A skip bin, delivered to your driveway and taken away when you're done.
  2. Taking it to the tip yourself, at the Albury Waste Management Centre, using your free digital waste vouchers.
  3. AlburyCity's home-based collection, for eligible residents who can't get hard waste to the centre themselves.

Which one makes sense depends on how much you have, what it is, and whether you have a trailer and the time to do the runs.

Option 1: a skip bin

In short: A skip suits jobs with a lot of mixed rubbish, heavy waste or many trailer loads of garden waste, because you load it at your own pace and it's taken away in one go.

A skip sits on your driveway (or on the street with council approval) for an agreed hire period. You can fill it over a few days, and you don't need a trailer, a ute or anyone to lift heavy items. Skips can take things the voucher loads and home-based collection won't, such as building waste, bricks, soil, concrete and large amounts of mixed rubbish, as long as you choose the right bin and tell the operator. They can't take asbestos, gas bottles, batteries, tyres or chemicals in a normal load. See what can go in a skip and the size guide.

For a deceased estate, a house move or a big shed or garage job, see our clean-outs guide.

Option 2: the Albury Waste Management Centre and your vouchers

In short: If you have a trailer and only a load or two, you can take household and garden waste to the Albury Waste Management Centre, and your four free vouchers a year can cover it.

AlburyCity's Albury Waste Management Centre is at 565 Mudge Street, Hamilton Valley. AlburyCity lists it as open 7am to 5pm Monday to Friday and 9am to 4pm on weekends, closed on Good Friday and Christmas Day. It's a drive-through site: you enter over the weighbridge and drop each material in its own area. Fees depend on the type and weight of material, so separating reusables and recyclables before you arrive can lower what you pay. It also has the Albury Recycling Centre and the Albury Upcycle and Recycle Shop.

Homeowners and renters can get four digital waste and recycling vouchers every 12 months through AlburyCity's Online Resident Services. AlburyCity says each voucher covers up to 500 kg of garden and general household waste. They can't be used for commercial or industrial waste, tyres or asbestos, and you need to separate recyclables from general waste.

Option 3: AlburyCity's home-based collection

In short: If you can't get hard waste to the centre yourself, AlburyCity may collect it from your front yard once a year, using two of your vouchers.

AlburyCity's home-based collection is for eligible residents who can't transport hard waste themselves, even with support, and evidence may be required. Eligible residents get one collection in a rolling 12-month period, and two vouchers are surrendered when you book. Items stay just inside your front yard, not on the nature strip.

  • It takes: household furniture, small appliances, tools and yard items, kitchenware, rolled carpet, mattresses and bases, and branches and prunings under 1.8 m. The total can't be bigger than 1 m high, 2 m wide and 2 m deep.
  • It won't take: building and demolition waste, bricks, concrete, soil, liquids and chemicals, hazardous waste including asbestos, tyres, batteries, gas bottles, steel and large whitegoods, and items two people can't lift.

What about my kerbside bins?

In short: Your bins are for everyday rubbish, recycling and food and garden waste, not clean-ups.

Albury households have a standard three-bin service: a green-lid organics bin for food and garden waste collected every week, and a red-lid general waste bin and a yellow-lid recycling bin collected on alternating fortnights. AlburyCity says items too large for your bins can be taken to the Albury Waste Management Centre.

So which is easier?

In short: Pick a skip for big, mixed or heavy jobs. Use your vouchers at the centre for a load or two of household or garden waste.

  • A skip bin usually makes sense for a full clean-out, a renovation, a big garden or shed job, heavy waste, or when you don't have a trailer or the time for several tip runs.
  • The Albury Waste Management Centre suits one or two trailer loads, especially while you still have vouchers.
  • The home-based collection suits eligible residents with a small amount of household or green waste.

Frequently asked questions

What is the easiest way to get rid of a lot of rubbish in Albury?

It depends on how much you have and what it is. For one or two loads, a trailer run to the Albury Waste Management Centre, using your free digital waste vouchers, may be enough. For a big clean-out, heavy waste or many loads, a skip bin is often the easier option, because you load it at your own pace and it's taken away in one trip.

Where is the Albury tip and when is it open?

AlburyCity's Albury Waste Management Centre is at 565 Mudge Street, Hamilton Valley. AlburyCity lists it as open 7am to 5pm on weekdays and 9am to 4pm on weekends, closed on Good Friday and Christmas Day.

How do AlburyCity's waste vouchers work?

Homeowners and renters can get four digital vouchers every 12 months through Online Resident Services. Each covers up to 500 kg of garden and general household waste at the Albury Waste Management Centre. They can't be used for commercial waste, tyres or asbestos.

Does AlburyCity pick up bulky rubbish?

Only for eligible residents who can't transport hard waste themselves. AlburyCity's home-based collection is once in a rolling 12 months, uses two vouchers, and the total load can't be bigger than 1 m high, 2 m wide and 2 m deep.

Can I use my Albury waste vouchers for a skip bin?

No. AlburyCity's vouchers are for its Albury Waste Management Centre and its home-based collection. Private skip hire isn't one of the listed uses.

Can a skip bin take garden waste and building rubbish?

Usually, yes, which is one reason people choose a skip. Heavy waste such as soil, concrete and bricks normally needs a smaller bin with a weight limit, and asbestos can never go in a general skip. Tell the operator what you have when you request a quote.

Do I need a permit for a skip bin on the street in Albury?

A skip wholly on your own property generally doesn't need council approval. A skip on the road, footpath or verge usually needs approval from AlburyCity. See our council skip permits guide for details.
